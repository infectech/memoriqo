# Master Website Prompt — Memoriqo
## An Artistic, Premium Digital-Gifting Experience

### 1. Project Overview

Design and build a complete, production-quality landing page for **Memoriqo**, a premium personalized digital-experience brand that transforms meaningful moments into beautiful, interactive, shareable websites.

Memoriqo allows people to create thoughtful digital gifts for the people they love. Customers choose an occasion, select a visual theme, share their photos and messages, and receive a personalized website created especially for their recipient.

Occasions include:
- Birthday surprises
- Romantic anniversaries
- Love stories and relationship milestones
- Proposals and Valentine's Day
- Friendship celebrations
- Graduation and personal achievements
- Wedding invitations and celebrations
- Personalized letters, memories, and other meaningful occasions

The product is not merely a website. It is a personal experience, a digital keepsake, and a gift someone can open, explore, and remember.

**Primary brand idea:** Every meaningful moment deserves a beautiful place to live.

**Brand name:** Memoriqo

**Brand category:** Personalized digital gifts and interactive occasion websites.

**Brand personality:** Artistic, premium, intimate, thoughtful, elegant, imaginative, and modern.

**Target audience:** Young adults, couples, friends, families, and people looking for a memorable, personal alternative to conventional gifts. The initial market is Bangladesh, with a visual identity and experience suitable for international audiences.

**Business model:** Customers choose a theme or a custom package, submit their content, and Memoriqo creates and delivers their personalized website.

### 2. Mandatory Skill and Workflow Instructions

Before starting the design or implementation:

1. Inspect the available skills and activate every relevant skill for frontend development, UI/UX design, creative direction, responsive web design, accessibility, interaction design, animation, SEO, performance optimization, and conversion-focused landing pages.
2. Follow the relevant skill instructions instead of relying on generic website templates.
3. If a dedicated design, website-building, or frontend skill is available, use it for its intended purpose.
4. Establish the visual direction, component hierarchy, typography, color system, interaction patterns, and responsive behavior before implementation.
5. If brand assets, design references, or project files are provided, inspect them and use the actual assets. Never recreate a supplied logo with ordinary text or substitute an incorrect logo.
6. Build and verify the actual working landing page, not just a static mockup or a description of the design.
7. Test the finished experience on desktop, tablet, and mobile. Correct layout, interaction, accessibility, and performance problems before considering the task complete.

Use the tools and skills available in the current environment. Do not claim to have run a skill, test, or deployment that was not actually performed.

### 3. Creative Direction: An Editorial Art Piece Meets a Digital Love Letter

The website must feel like a carefully art-directed independent design studio combined with a premium lifestyle brand.

Think of the atmosphere of:
- An elegant independent editorial magazine
- A beautifully composed handwritten letter
- A contemporary boutique stationery brand
- An intimate photo album
- A thoughtfully designed digital keepsake
- A modern, cinematic portfolio with restrained motion

These are aesthetic references, not templates to copy.

The result must feel original, emotionally engaging, and unmistakably Memoriqo.

**The most important design principle:** The website should make visitors feel something before asking them to buy something.

Avoid the conventional SaaS aesthetic: no generic gradient hero, rows of identical white cards, excessive rounded rectangles, predictable icon grids, or an oversized dashboard mockup dominating the page.

Avoid making the website look like a wedding-only business. It must feel equally appropriate for birthdays, friendships, graduations, romantic moments, and family celebrations.

### 4. Visual Identity

#### Color palette

Build a cohesive, warm, editorial palette around:

- Warm ivory: `#FAF7F1`
- Paper white: `#FFFDF9`
- Ink black: `#24221F`
- Muted rose: `#D7A6A0`
- Dusty mauve: `#BBA6B9`
- Soft sage: `#A9B5A0`
- Warm taupe: `#C6B9A9`

Use ivory and ink as the foundation. Use rose, mauve, sage, and taupe selectively to distinguish sections and occasion themes.

The page should feel warm and tactile rather than stark, glossy, or overly pink.

Do not use every accent color everywhere. Establish a consistent primary palette, then use occasion-specific colors in the gallery and product previews.

#### Typography

Create a refined typographic system with expressive contrast.

- **Display typography:** A sophisticated editorial serif with graceful letterforms, ideally something like Instrument Serif, Cormorant Garamond, or an equivalent available font.
- **Body typography:** A clean, highly readable sans-serif such as Manrope, Inter, or a similar typeface.
- **Optional handwritten accent:** Use sparingly for tiny annotations, personal notes, or decorative microcopy. It must remain readable.

Use oversized, elegant headlines, generous line height, carefully controlled text widths, and deliberate changes in scale.

Typography should be a primary design element, not merely a way to display information.

Keep the body copy concise and the visual hierarchy unmistakable.

#### Textures and decorative details

Use subtle design details such as:
- Fine paper grain
- Delicate film-grain texture
- Thin editorial rules
- Small handwritten annotations
- Organic photo crops
- Soft, natural shadows
- Carefully layered photographs
- Delicate borders and restrained corner treatments
- Tiny star, flower, or heart motifs used only where appropriate

Texture should be extremely subtle and should never compromise readability or performance.

Do not cover the entire page in decorative elements. Negative space is essential.

### 6. Hero Section — The Emotional First Impression

This is the most important section on the page.

Create an artistic, immersive first screen that communicates the feeling of giving someone a personalized digital experience.

Use an asymmetrical editorial composition rather than a conventional centered headline with three floating cards.

Possible composition:
- A large, expressive headline occupying one side of the screen.
- A layered visual composition on the other side, showing fragments of beautifully designed personalized websites.
- A photograph in an organic or editorial crop.
- A small overlapping letter, date, photograph, or keepsake detail.
- Tiny annotations that suggest a personal story.
- Delicate motion that makes the composition feel alive.

The layout must remain beautiful at common mobile screen sizes. Do not simply shrink the desktop composition.

**Suggested hero copy:**

Eyebrow:
A LITTLE SOMETHING, MADE JUST FOR THEM

Headline:
**Some feelings deserve more than a message.**

Supporting copy:
Turn your favorite memories, heartfelt words, and special moments into a beautiful little website made just for someone you love.

Primary CTA:
Create a Gift

Secondary CTA:
Explore the Experiences

Supporting microcopy:
Personalized with your photos, your words, your story.

Alternative headline to consider:
**A world of memories. Made just for one person.**

Choose the stronger headline based on the overall composition. Do not display every headline alternative simultaneously.

The CTA must lead into a real, clearly defined creation or inquiry journey. If a complete checkout or personalization system is not implemented, route the action to a working inquiry or order-start flow rather than a dead button.

#### Hero visual direction

The main visual should resemble a beautiful editorial collage or a small gallery of personal digital experiences.

Include realistic website-preview compositions, such as:
- A romantic anniversary page with a timeline and personal photographs.
- A playful birthday surprise with a message and celebratory details.
- A soft, nostalgic friendship memory page.
- An elegant wedding invitation.

These should look like genuine examples of the product, not generic illustrations of laptops and phones.

Use real photography or carefully selected, legally usable imagery where available. Use consistent image treatment and intentional crops.

The visual should communicate that the recipient will open a beautiful experience, not that the customer is buying website-development services.

#### Gallery art direction

Do not use six identical cards with an icon, heading, and paragraph.

Instead:
- Use an editorial grid with varied image proportions.
- Mix portrait, landscape, and square previews.
- Use intentional whitespace and slightly varied section composition.
- Give each occasion its own visual personality while maintaining the same brand identity.
- Use high-quality photographic imagery and actual sample-page previews.
- Include a short title and a clear action such as Explore Birthday Themes.

Hover interactions can reveal an additional detail or a more complete preview. On touch devices, the same content must remain accessible without hover.

Each experience should link to its relevant theme gallery or order flow.

If individual category pages are not yet implemented, use functional anchors or a working filter interface rather than broken links.

### 18. Motion and Interaction Design

Motion should feel delicate, natural, and intentional.

Use:
- Gentle fade-and-rise entrances for selected content.
- Slow, subtle movement in the hero collage.
- Small image parallax effects where they add genuine depth.
- Elegant hover transitions on gallery previews.
- Smooth but restrained section transitions.
- Subtle text reveals when they improve the storytelling.
- A polished mobile menu and accessible accordion.
- Responsive CTA interactions with clear feedback.

Motion must support the emotional narrative rather than compete with it.

Avoid:
- Constantly moving elements.
- Excessive scroll hijacking.
- Large animated backgrounds.
- Aggressive cursor effects.
- Long intro animations before the page becomes usable.
- Animations that make reading difficult.
- Mandatory autoplay audio or video.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Ensure that all essential content is immediately available when animations are disabled.

### 19. Responsive Design

Design for mobile first, then enhance the desktop experience.

The page must work beautifully at:
- Small mobile screens.
- Standard mobile screens.
- Tablets.
- Laptops.
- Large desktop displays.

On mobile:
- Preserve the expressive typography without awkward wrapping.
- Recompose the hero collage rather than simply shrinking it.
- Use appropriately cropped images.
- Keep navigation and CTAs accessible.
- Prevent horizontal overflow.
- Maintain generous but practical spacing.
- Make interactive targets comfortably touchable.
- Avoid excessive decorative motion.
- Keep text readable without zooming.

The mobile experience is especially important because customers will likely discover the brand through social media links and messaging apps.

### 20. Technical and Implementation Requirements

Use the existing project stack if one has already been established. Otherwise, choose a modern, maintainable, production-ready frontend architecture suitable for a small commercial website.

Requirements:
- Reusable components.
- Clear component and file organization.
- Centralized design tokens.
- Maintainable content data for experiences, themes, FAQs, and prices.
- Responsive image handling.
- Semantic HTML.
- Accessible navigation and interactive controls.
- Meaningful loading and error states where needed.
- Clean URLs and appropriate page metadata.
- No unnecessary dependencies.
- No placeholder buttons that appear functional but do nothing.
- No fabricated forms, successful submissions, or checkout confirmations.

If a backend is not available, do not pretend that orders or messages have been stored. Connect the primary CTAs to an actual configured inquiry or ordering mechanism where possible, or clearly identify the integration required.

Do not introduce a complex CMS, authentication system, or automated order-management platform merely to build this landing page.

The architecture should be lightweight and easy to maintain as Memoriqo grows.

### 22. Performance and Accessibility

Prioritize a fast, polished experience.

- Optimize and appropriately size images.
- Use modern image formats when supported.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery.
- Avoid layout shifts.
- Keep JavaScript and animation libraries lightweight.
- Load only necessary font weights.
- Maintain sufficient text contrast.
- Support keyboard navigation.
- Use visible focus indicators.
- Give controls clear accessible names.
- Use accessible accordion and menu semantics.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Do not make essential content dependent on animation or hover.

Aim for strong Core Web Vitals and excellent Lighthouse results, but do not claim a score unless it has been measured.

### 23. Design Quality Bar

Before completion, review the entire page against these criteria:

**Art direction:** Does the page look intentionally designed, rather than assembled from a generic component library?

**Emotional resonance:** Does it communicate the feeling of creating a personal gift?

**Brand distinctiveness:** Could the page be recognized as Memoriqo without relying on the logo alone?

**Visual hierarchy:** Is the most important message clear within seconds?

**Product clarity:** Does the visitor understand what they receive, how it works, and what to do next?

**Visual consistency:** Do typography, photography, spacing, color, and motion work as one coherent system?

**Mobile quality:** Does the mobile layout feel art-directed rather than compressed?

**Functional completeness:** Do navigation links, theme previews, FAQs, and CTAs work as intended?

**Trust:** Are prices, policies, examples, and claims honest and appropriately qualified?

**Performance:** Does the site load quickly and remain usable without unnecessary animations?

If any section feels generic, repetitive, or visually weak, redesign it before delivery.

Deliver a complete, responsive, polished landing page for Memoriqo.

The result should feel like a premium creative brand that combines the intimacy of a handwritten letter, the beauty of an editorial magazine, and the delight of opening a personalized digital gift.

Make it artistic without being cluttered, premium without being cold, romantic without being limited to romance, and modern without looking like another generic SaaS startup.

**Final creative principle: Don't just show people a service. Make them imagine the feeling of giving someone a Memoriqo.**
